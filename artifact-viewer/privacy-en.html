Privacy Policy

makkopai (the "Operator") sets out below how user information is handled in "Artifact Viewer" (the "App"), an app for iOS, iPadOS and macOS.

Effective date: November 13, 2026

1. Our Approach

The App saves HTML files made with AI services and other tools ("artifacts") on your device and shows them.

  • You can use the App without creating an account.
  • The contents, titles and file names of the artifacts you add, and the data artifacts save on your device, are not sent to the Operator's servers. They are stored on your device. If you turn on iCloud sync, they are also stored in your own iCloud (section 2).
  • The Operator does not collect your name, email address, phone number, location, contacts, photos or similar information (except emails you send us; section 11).

To provide its features, the App stores the following on your device. None of it is sent to the Operator.

iCloud sync
If you turn on "Sync with iCloud" in Settings, artifacts, artifact details and folders from the table above are stored in your private area of Apple's iCloud (the CloudKit private database) and synced between devices that use the same Apple ID.

  • They are stored in your iCloud, and the Operator cannot see them. They use your iCloud storage.
  • Thumbnails, page data, display settings and records for Library Insights and the Health Check are not synced.
  • Information in iCloud is handled under Apple's privacy policy.

How to delete

  • Deleting an artifact in the App deletes its file, thumbnail and page data. If iCloud sync is on, it is also deleted from iCloud and from your other synced devices.
  • Deleting the App from a device deletes all of the App's information on that device. Data saved in iCloud can be deleted from the iCloud settings in your device's Settings app.

3. Communication by Artifacts Themselves

Depending on who or which AI service made it, an artifact may be built to communicate with outside servers. For example, it may:

  • Load scripts, images or fonts from outside (CDNs, Google Fonts and so on)
  • Embed outside services such as maps or videos
  • Send data from inside the artifact to an outside web service

In these cases, as when you view a web page in a browser, your IP address, device and browser type and similar information are sent to those destinations. Whether such communication happens, and what it contains, depends on the artifact's contents. The Operator does not control it and is not responsible for how information is handled at those destinations. Please check each destination's privacy policy.

Links inside an artifact open in your device's default browser (such as Safari), not in the App.

4. Information the Operator Receives

The only information the Operator receives, or that is sent outside through mechanisms the Operator built into the App, is described in sections 5 (advertising) and 6 (usage statistics).

In addition, when a user who has purchased the Paid Features views "Community Trends", the App downloads a file of aggregated numbers that the Operator stores on Google Cloud Storage. As with loading any web page, this sends information needed for the connection, such as your IP address, to Google LLC. The Operator does not use records of this connection to analyze users.

To show announcements from the Operator (such as new features or updates), the App also loads an announcement file that the Operator places on GitHub Pages, when it starts and at similar times (at most once every 12 hours). As with loading any web page, this sends information needed for the connection, such as your IP address and device and OS type, to GitHub, Inc. No information that identifies you is sent, and the Operator does not receive records of this connection. The announcement file carries the Operator's digital signature, and the App shows only announcements whose signature it can verify.

5. Advertising

On iPhone and iPad, the App uses Google AdMob, an ad service provided by Google LLC, to show the following ads. No ads are shown on Mac. If you have purchased the Paid Features, no ads are shown and AdMob is not started.

  • An ad at the bottom of the library screen (banner ad)
  • An ad you can choose to watch to increase how many artifacts you can save (rewarded ad)

No ads are shown on the screen that displays an artifact.

AdMob collects information such as the following to serve ads, measure their effectiveness and prevent fraud:

  • Your device's advertising identifier (IDFA), only if you allow it in iOS's "Allow Tracking" prompt
  • IP address, device type, OS version and language setting
  • Records of ads shown and of taps and other interactions

The App asks whether to allow tracking the first time you open it.

  • If you don't allow tracking, ads tailored to you (personalized ads) are not shown. Ads are still shown. Either way, all of the App's features work
  • You can change the tracking setting at any time in your device's Settings app

For how Google handles information, see Google's Privacy Policy and How Google uses information from sites or apps that use our services.

Information collected for ads is never combined with the usage statistics in section 6.

6. Usage Statistics

Only if you agree, the App sends usage statistics to improve the App and to produce statistics. You are asked when you have added 3 artifacts, or when you first open "Library Insights", and you can change your choice at any time in Settings. You can see what is sent on the consent screen and in Settings.

Usage statistics are sent with Google Analytics for Firebase, provided by Google LLC. Until you agree, it collects nothing.

What is sent

  • The kind of each artifact, converted into fixed choices (for example: estimated AI that made it, purpose and subject categories, language, kinds of technologies and libraries used, kinds of data handled, kinds of outside communication)
  • Numbers describing an artifact's scale (for example: lines, number of functions or input fields, characters, file size), rounded to two significant digits
  • A random identifier made on your device for each artifact. It is not made from the artifact's contents or file name
  • Rough ranges describing use (for example: how many times and on how many days it was opened in a week, viewing time, days since it was added, whether it is a favorite or in a folder, how it was added, theme setting, how long it was kept before deletion)
  • The kinds of problems the Health Check found (for example: content wider than the screen, text too small)
  • If you answer a "Quick Question", your answer (chosen from fixed options). Quick Questions can be turned off in Settings
  • User categories: whether you have purchased the Paid Features, a range for your library size, and whether iCloud sync is on
  • Information Google Analytics for Firebase collects automatically: an identifier assigned per app install (app instance ID), device type, OS and app versions, language setting, approximate region (estimated from your IP address), app launches and usage time, in-app purchases, and when data was sent

What is not sent

  • The contents, titles, file names or URLs of artifacts, page data, page text, or error messages
  • Advertising identifier (IDFA), identifier for vendor (IDFV), or location from Location Services
  • The addresses that artifacts communicate with (only their kinds are sent)

Usage statistics are not combined with the advertising information in section 5 and are not used to personalize ads.

If you turn off usage statistics, nothing more is sent and the app instance ID on your device is deleted. Because the Operator cannot identify you from the information received, it cannot find and delete the data of an individual user. For how Google handles information, see Google's Privacy Policy.

Community Trends
The Operator produces aggregated numbers from the usage statistics received (for example, the share of artifacts by AI) and shows them in the App's "Library Insights". Anyone can download the file containing these numbers, but categories with few users are left out.

Providing statistics to third parties
The Operator may combine data from many users into statistics that cannot identify any person or device, and provide them to third parties or use them in research and reports. Categories with so few users that they could lead to identification are not released.

Destination: Google LLC (Google Analytics for Firebase). The collected information is exported to BigQuery on Google Cloud, managed by the Operator, and aggregated there.
